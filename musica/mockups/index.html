Música

A player for the Spanish songs in Musica on the SD card, each an .mp3 with an .srt of the same name, whose lyrics follow along as the song plays. Two directions for review: A takes Spotify's look, B Apple Music's. Both are laid out for the XP3's 240×320 screen and its d-pad; the keys are in the table at the end.

A · Spotify

Spotify's dark library and Now Playing, with lyrics on a block of the cover's colour: sung lines white, the coming ones in black.

B · Apple Music

Apple Music's light library and its blurred-cover player. Only the line being sung is bright; the rest fade, and blur further away from it.

Left on the lyrics, starting the line again, is there for learning: you can hear a line as many times as you need without searching the progress bar for it. Nothing on screen spells the keys out, as in the other apps.

The files

  • SD card/Musica/Cielito lindo.mp3 + Cielito lindo.srt, paired by name. Subfolders are allowed and become albums when the tags have none.
  • Title, artist, album and cover come from the MP3's tags (MediaMetadataRetriever, built into Android). With no tags, Artist - Title.mp3 is split; with no cover, one is drawn from the title in a colour taken from it.
  • Each SRT cue is one lyric line, and a cue with two lines of text stays one block. A gap of 6 s or more between cues shows an instrumental break (♪ in A, Apple's three dots in B).
  • The lyrics' colour (A) and the blurred background (B) are taken from the cover (Android's Palette is a library, so this would be a small colour pick written for the app).
  • A song with no .srt still plays; it says so in its row and where the lyrics would be.

The lyrics in these mockups are “Cielito lindo”, a traditional song in the public domain. The covers are drawn; real ones come from the MP3s. Type: Figtree stands in for Spotify Mix, Inter for SF Pro (both OFL, bundled with the app).
